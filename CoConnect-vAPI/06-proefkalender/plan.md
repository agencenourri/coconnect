# vAPI Network: LinkedIn plan for weeks 1 and 2

**Prepared by:** Lotte Korevaar, for CoConnect
**Period:** 28 September to 9 October 2026
**Based on:** LinkedIn research of 26 September 2026 (sources mostly March to August 2026). Section numbers like (§4) refer to that research.

## 1. Summary

- The goal is conversations with decision-makers, waitlist signups and design partners. Likes and followers are tracked but aren't the goal.
- Mark's profile carries the business message and [FOUNDER 2: NAAM]'s profile carries the freelancer message. The company page supports both.
- There are 15 posts in two weeks, all inside three pillars: the agent economy, payments and escrow, and build in public.
- Every post starts with a problem the reader recognises. Stablecoins and escrow come in halfway, as the practical answer.
- The work around each post is part of the plan: a reaction block in the first hour and 15 to 20 minutes a day of comments on target accounts.

## 2. Choices and why

| Choice | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Founder profiles are the main channel | Personal profiles average 2.6% engagement against 1.6% for company pages. LinkedIn's ranking model (360Brew) also checks whether an author is credible on a topic, and a person builds that faster than a logo | §4 |
| Mark talks to businesses, founder 2 to freelancers | 360Brew needs to know who a profile is for. One profile speaking to two audiences gets shown well to neither. The main goal is B2B, so it goes to the founder with the most relevant profile | §3, §4 |
| Company page posts twice a week, mostly reshares | The brief gives the page a supporting role: sharing founder posts with its own comment, plus one milestone or announcement a week | §1 |
| Three pillars only | A profile that posts on too many topics can't be classified by the model, so reach drops | §3 |
| 3 posts per founder per week (founder 2 starts with 2) | 3 a week is the minimum that works, and quality matters more than volume. Founder 2 starts lighter in week 1 while the profile is set up | §5 |
| Weekly mix: 1 carousel, 1 opinion post, 1 build-in-public or podcast post | This is the research's recommended structure for weeks 2 to 4 | §5 |
| Carousels of 8 to 12 slides, as a step-by-step process | Document carousels are the top format in every dataset, and people save reference material. Saves are the strongest signal | §4, §6 |
| Text posts of 1,000 to 2,000 characters | Absolute interactions peak at 900 to 1,299 characters and the engagement rate rises with length. This range gets both | §6 |
| No polls. Video only as a podcast clip | Polls are among the weakest formats for small accounts. Video views on pages fell 36% in a year | §6 |
| Links only in the first comment, and never the point of a post | The link penalty is disputed (one source says 60% less reach, another found none), so the content always stands on its own | §4 |
| Stablecoins framed as a cost decision | Business buyers care about speed, cost and reliability. The posts use sourced fees (Request Finance, 2026) and the GENIUS Act and MiCA. There's nothing about tokens, prices or "web3" | §8 |
| No invented facts | Anything the research doesn't confirm is a visible placeholder. The scoped-jobs product isn't named until Rik confirms it's Tasks | §1, §2 |
| Every post ends with a real question or a DM offer | Three substantive comments count for more than thirty likes. "Agree?" or "Thoughts?" on their own get ignored | §4, §7 |
| One lead magnet, the One-Page Scope | Only people who commission or do scoped work ask for it, and it explains how escrow works. Each request starts a DM conversation, which counts as a lead | §10 |

## 3. Targets

**Starting assumptions:** each founder has 500 to 2,000 connections and posts rarely. The company page has fewer than 500 followers. I'll recalibrate after week 1 if the real numbers are different.

The research is clear that B2B tech audiences scroll fast and rarely comment, so low comment counts are normal. The first organic lead usually comes after 60 to 90 days (§10). Early conversations will mostly come from the daily engagement work, not from posts alone.

### What counts as a lead
- **A (qualified):** a decision-maker at a company that outsources work, builds agents or offers APIs, who starts or accepts a DM conversation about early access, a pilot or how they hire. On the freelancer side, a freelancer or agent builder with a relevant skill who asks for early access.
- **B (warm):** fits the target profile and has commented twice or asked for the scope template.
- **C (signal):** a like or comment from outside the target profile. Tracked, never reported as a lead.

The weekly report leads with A and B leads. Reach comes last.

## 4. Actions around the posts

The full day-by-day schedule is in `kalender.md`. The rules behind it:

| Action | Rule | Why | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proofread the evening before | Nothing gets edited in the first hour | One source says an edit resets the test phase. That isn't widely confirmed, but it costs nothing to avoid | §11 |
| Warm up before posting | Comment on 3 to 5 target posts in the 15 minutes before | The account is active and visible when the test phase starts | §4 |
| Reaction block | Reply to every comment within 15 minutes, in 2 to 3 sentences, for at least the first hour | A new post is first tested on 2% to 5% of the network. Replies in that hour decide whether it goes further | §4 |
| Daily engagement | 15 to 20 minutes on a fixed list of 30 to 50 target accounts | The research calls this the tactic with the most leverage, and almost nobody does it systematically | §5 |
| Same-day DMs | Every target-profile commenter and every "scope" or "early" request | Conversations are the lead definition, so they need a follow-up | §10 |
| No pods or bought engagement | Only real comments from the founders' own accounts | Pods are detected and penalised | §4 |
| Weekly report | Every Friday to Baha: leads first, then signals, then reach, then what I'll change | The measures in section 3 are only useful if someone reads them each week | §10 |

### DM scripts

After a "scope" request:
> Hi [name], here's the one-page scope: [link]. Out of curiosity, how often do you hire freelancers or agents at [company]? I'm collecting stories about what goes wrong in practice.

After a comment from a target profile:
> Thanks for your comment on [topic]. Your point about [x] stuck with me. Do you mostly outsource [type of work]? We're opening early access to a small group of businesses before launch, and I'd value your view.

After an "early" request:
> Thanks for getting in touch. What do you do, or what does your agent do? We're putting together a first group before launch and I'll make sure you're on the list.

A-leads go to the early-access list and to CoConnect's outreach team the same day, and each one gets a line in the lead tracker.

## 5. Visuals

The vAPI brand is dark, with an orange line and classic opera paintings. Hardly anyone posting about hiring or payments looks like that, so the covers are what makes people stop scrolling.

| Element | What I'll do | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Carousel covers | A crop of a classic painting, darkened, with the hook in large type and the orange line | It's recognisable in the feed and matches the brand |
| Inner slides | Dark background, one point per slide, at most 25 words, body text at least 28pt. The orange line grows a little on each slide as a progress bar | Easy to read on a phone, and the growing line keeps people swiping |
| Diagrams (escrow, disputes, payment route) | The orange line is the path the money takes | It explains the product visually without a sales pitch |
| Founder posts | Mostly text. Real photos only, no stock images | Research: an original voice and specific details perform better, and generic content is pushed down (§4) |
| Podcast clips | 30 to 90 seconds, subtitles burned in, dark bar with the orange line and the guest's name | Most people watch without sound (§9) |
| Company page | More brand: artwork and the orange line | The page is the reference point people land on |
| Formats | Carousels 1080×1350 as PDF. Images 1080×1350 | Portrait takes up the most space in the mobile feed |
| Rights and accessibility | Public-domain art only (Rijksmuseum, The Met Open Access, Wikimedia Commons), with the licence checked for each work. Alt text on every image | Avoids licence problems, and makes the posts readable for everyone |

**Canva master template:** six slide types (cover, statement, numbered step, diagram, quote, CTA). Every carousel is built from these, so production is fast and every post looks like vAPI.

## 6. Assumptions

1. Both founders agree to post from their own profiles. Either I get access, or they publish the scheduled posts themselves and do the reaction blocks with my replies.
2. The audience is mostly English-speaking US and Europe, so all times are in ET.
3. No post mentions tokens, prices or speculation.
4. Numbers about vAPI (users, volume, clients) only appear once the team supplies them.
