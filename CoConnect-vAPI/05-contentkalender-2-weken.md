## 1. Strategy

### 1.1 Why the founders carry it, not the logo
LinkedIn ranks posts partly by reading the author's profile and posting history, and checking whether this person is credible on this topic. A founder who posts about one subject builds that credibility. A logo builds very little of it, and company pages mostly reach people who already follow them. People also reply to people. A comment on a founder's post turns into a DM conversation. A comment on a company page usually goes nowhere.

### 1.2 Why businesses and freelancers are split over two founders
LinkedIn learns who to show each profile to. If one profile talks to hiring managers on Tuesday and freelancers on Wednesday, it gets shown to neither group well. So each founder gets one audience:

| Account | Audience | Job | Posts/week |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Founder A** | Founders, COOs, ops and product leads, agency owners who outsource work | Start conversations with businesses (main goal) | 3 |
| **Founder B** | Freelancers, specialists, AI-agent builders | Build the supply side and credibility with talent | 2 |
| **Company page** | Everyone who lands on vAPI from either founder | Explain clearly what vAPI Network is | 1 |

**Why businesses sit with a founder and not with the company page:** the company page posts once a week and reaches mostly existing followers. The main goal needs the most reach, so it goes to the account with the most reach.

### 1.3 Messaging rules
1. **Lead with the problem, never the protocol.** The first two lines are about something every buyer or freelancer recognises: the freelancer who vanished at 80%, the invoice stuck "with finance". Escrow and stablecoin payment come in halfway, as proof.
2. **No token, no prices, no "web3" as a selling point** on LinkedIn. Business buyers care about speed, cost and reliability. That conversation belongs on X.
3. **Pre-launch honesty.** All products are "coming soon". We never say something is live. We talk about what we're building and why, and invite people in early.
4. **Name clarity.** "Vapi" search results are dominated by a voice-AI company. We always write **vAPI Network**, and the company page says outright that it's a different company.

### 1.4 Content pillars

| Pillar | Founder A (businesses) | Founder B (freelancers & builders) | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. How work gets commissioned** | Why outsourcing fails, scoping, fixed price vs hourly | Getting paid, scope creep, payment terms | ~50% |
| **2. The agent economy** | Hiring an AI agent like you hire a contractor | Turning an agent into income | ~25% |
| **3. Build in public** | Design decisions (disputes, review) | How payment works, step by step | ~25% |

### 1.5 Formats
- **Carousel (PDF document):** the main format for explaining things, one per founder per week. Before launch, explaining is all we have, and carousels also keep people on the post longer (dwell time).
- **Text post:** stories and opinions. No image, or a real photo of the founder. For personal profiles, a real face beats a graphic.
- **Single image / diagram:** company page, and one founder post on how the dispute flow works.
- **Rules:** no external links in the post (the link goes in the Featured section and in DMs), no polls, no "comment YES" bait. Text posts are 1,000–2,000 characters.

### 1.6 How a post becomes a lead

```
Post → comment → reply within 15 min with a question → profile visit
     → profile works as a landing page (Featured: early access + scope template)
     → DM → qualify (A/B/C) → early-access list / call with CoConnect outreach
```

**Lead magnet: The One-Page Scope.** It's a one-page template: outcome, deliverables, acceptance criteria, price and deadline, and what happens if it goes wrong. Posts offer it with *"DM me 'scope'"*. It works for three reasons:
- It's genuinely useful, so people share it.
- It filters: only people who commission scoped work, or do it, want this.
- It *is* how vAPI works, so every request comes from someone who now understands the product.

**CTA ladder:** about 1 in 3 posts has a direct CTA (DM for the template or early access). The rest end with a question that only the ideal client can answer well. The people who answer are the ones we follow up with.

### 1.7 What counts as a lead
| Grade | Business side (Founder A) | Talent side (Founder B) |
|---|---|---|
| **A: qualified** | A decision-maker at a company that outsources regularly, who starts or accepts a DM conversation about how they hire, or about early access | A freelancer or agent builder with a relevant skill who asks for early access |
| **B: warm** | Fits the ideal-client profile, and has engaged 2+ times or requested the template | Requested the template or engaged 2+ times |
| **C: signal** | A like or comment from outside the ideal-client profile | Same |

Only A and B are reported as leads. C is tracked, never celebrated.

## 2. Targets: what to realistically expect

**Starting assumptions.** Each founder has roughly 500–2,000 connections and little recent posting history. The company page has fewer than 500 followers. If the real numbers are very different, I recalibrate after week 1.

**How reach works, so the numbers make sense.** Each post is first shown to a small slice of the author's network. If that group reacts with real comments and time spent reading, it gets shown to more people. If they don't, it stops. For a new or quiet account, LinkedIn is still learning who this person is relevant to. So weeks 1–2 are **calibration**: we find out which hooks and topics land with the right people. Reach grows in months 2 and 3, once the profile has a clear topic.

Likes, follower count and viral reach. A post with 400 impressions that produces two qualified conversations beats a post with 20,000 impressions that produces none. The weekly report leads with conversations, not reach.

#### A6 · Founder A · Thu 15 Oct · Text + dispute diagram
One of the hardest design decisions we've made at vAPI Network: what happens when a client and a freelancer disagree?

Every marketplace hits this moment. The work is delivered. The client says it isn't what they asked for. The freelancer says it is. Money is on the line.

On most platforms, the platform decides. That creates a quiet conflict of interest, because the platform needs both sides to keep coming back.

We went a different way:
→ the dispute goes to three independent reviewers
→ they check the work against the scope both sides signed
→ when two of them agree, that's the outcome: pay out, refund or split

Why three? One reviewer is a single point of failure. Two can deadlock. Three gives a clear majority without turning every disagreement into a court case.

And why does it work at all? Because the scope was signed upfront and the money was already in escrow. The reviewers aren't judging who's the nicer person. They're checking the work against a document.

We're still building this, and I'd really like input from people who've been on either side of a dispute.

If you run a company that outsources work and want to help shape this before launch, send me a DM. We're opening early access to a small group of businesses.

## 5. Engagement playbook: the daily work

Posting is 30% of the result. What happens around the post is the other 70%.

### 5.1 Daily routine

| When | Action | Time |
|---|---|---|
| **15 min before posting** | Warm-up: comment on 3–5 posts from target accounts, so the founder's account is active when the post goes live | 10 min |
| **Post goes live** | Check formatting, then post the first comment if needed (links only on the company page) | 2 min |
| **First 60 minutes** | Reply to **every** comment within 15 min. Each reply adds a thought or asks a follow-up question, never just "thanks!". If the commenter fits the ideal-client profile: view their profile and log them in the tracker | 20–30 min |
| **Daily, fixed slot** | Targeted commenting: 5–8 posts from the target list (5.3). 2–4 sentences that add something: an experience, a number, a counterpoint. Never pitch vAPI in someone else's comments | 20 min |
| **Daily** | DMs: follow up with template/early-access requests, anyone who commented twice, and profile visitors who fit the profile (connection request with a note, max 10–15 a day) | 10–15 min |
| **End of day** | Update the lead tracker: new leads, grades, next steps | 5 min |
| **Friday** | Weekly report (5.5) + schedule the next week | 45 min |

**Time per day:** about 60–75 minutes on posting days and 35–45 minutes on other days, across both founder accounts.

### 5.2 Rules for replying and commenting
- Reply within 15 minutes during the first hour. Early conversation decides whether the post gets shown further.
- Every reply ends with a question, when it fits naturally. A thread of comments counts for more than a list of separate ones.
- No engagement pods and no "great post!". Both are recognised and penalised.
- A comment from an A- or B-profile gets a DM that same day (scripts below).

## 6. Visuals: what I'll do and why

### 6.1 Principles
vAPI's brand is dark, with an orange line and classic opera artwork. Nobody in hiring or freelance content looks like that. Most competitors use stock photos of laptops and handshakes. That difference is our scroll-stopper, so I lean into it, but only where it helps the message.

| Account | Visual character |
|---|---|
| **Founders** | Human first: real photos of the founder, and carousels in the brand style. Few graphics on text posts, because on a personal profile a face beats a design |
| **Company page** | Brand first: opera artwork, the orange line, a clear explanation |

### 6.2 The visual system
- **Carousel covers:** a crop of a classic painting or opera scene, darkened, with the hook in large type and the orange line. The concept pairs a classic scene with a modern problem: a dramatic painted figure waiting under *"The invoice is with finance"*. The contrast is what makes people stop.
- **Inner slides:** no artwork. Dark background, one statement per slide, max 25 words, large type (body min. 28pt). The **orange line works as a progress bar** that grows a little on every slide, so readers see how far along they are and keep swiping.
- **Diagrams** (escrow flow, dispute flow): the orange line literally *is* the path the money takes. Steps as simple blocks along the line, with the dispute as a branch.
- **CTA slide:** the founder's photo, name, and one line: *DM me "scope"*. People reply to a face, not to a logo.
- **Typography:** vAPI's website fonts. If those aren't available: a classic serif for headlines (to echo the opera artwork) and a clean sans-serif for body text.
- **Formats:** carousels 1080×1350 (portrait, as PDF), single images 1080×1350 or 1200×1200.
- **Accessibility:** alt text on every image, and a contrast check on orange over dark.
- **Image rights:** public-domain artwork only (e.g. Rijksmuseum, The Met Open Access, Wikimedia Commons), with the licence checked per piece.

### 6.3 Canva master template
Six slide types, reused in every carousel: **cover · statement · numbered point · diagram · quote · CTA**. That makes new carousels fast and every post recognisably vAPI.
